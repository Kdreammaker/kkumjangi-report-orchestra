# Forbidden Patterns

## Stock Or Public Hype

- `혁신적인`, `업계를 선도`, `압도적`, `게임체인저`, `세계 최초` without approved evidence.
- Report-summary language that reads like internal analysis.
- Generic quote language that no named approver has cleared.

## Genre Drift

- Turning an internal risk memo into a public announcement.
- Exposing unapproved partner discussions or internal decision reasons.
- Treating draft facts, roadmap ideas, or negotiation status as public facts.

## Overclaim

- Market leadership, legal approval, performance, user adoption, revenue, partnership, or launch status without approved source.
- `공식 확정`, `승인 완료`, `계약 체결` unless the approval record supports it.

## Protected Content

- Do not rewrite approved quotes, official names, dates, figures, boilerplate, contact details, embargo text, legal disclaimers, or approval wording.
- Do not paraphrase public disclosure language if legal/comms approval depends on exact wording.

## Style-Pass Risk Labels

- Use `style-only` for local clarity, rhythm, headline concision, or connector issues.
- Use `evidence-related` when a public fact, market claim, performance claim, launch status, or attribution may change.
- Use `approval-sensitive` for quotes, boilerplate, contact, embargo, legal/disclosure, regulator, public, disclaimer, or comms-approved wording.
- Use `genre-drift` when a public statement becomes internal analysis, sales copy, or unapproved marketing.
- Use `reader-fit` when the public reader cannot distinguish approved facts, attribution, scope, and announcement status.
