# Forbidden Patterns

## Stock Or Over-Explaining

- Long abstract definitions before the learner knows why they matter.
- Repeated `쉽게 말해`, `한마디로`, `즉` without concrete examples.
- Excessive cheerful tone that makes serious or safety-related content feel unserious.

## Genre Drift

- Turning education material into a marketing page.
- Turning a fact lesson into a moral lecture.
- Removing necessary terms so the explanation becomes inaccurate.

## Overclaim

- `항상`, `절대`, `누구나 바로`, `완벽히 이해`.
- Claims that imply a child can perform a regulated, unsafe, or adult-supervised action alone.

## Protected Content

- Do not rewrite direct quotes, numbers, scientific names, law names, source-backed facts, safety warnings, approval text, or official instructions.
- Do not simplify a protected fact into a different fact.

## Style-Pass Risk Labels

- Use `style-only` for local wording, rhythm, connector, or explanation-sequence issues.
- Use `evidence-related` when simplification, examples, or analogies may change a fact or claim type.
- Use `approval-sensitive` for safety, legal, official instruction, public, disclaimer, or approved wording.
- Use `genre-drift` when learning material becomes marketing copy, moral lecture, or unsupported advice.
- Use `reader-fit` when vocabulary, pacing, example choice, or seriousness does not fit the learner.
