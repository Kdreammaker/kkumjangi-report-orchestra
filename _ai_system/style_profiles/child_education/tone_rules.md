# Tone Rules

## Reader Fit

- Use easy words and short sentences.
- Explain one idea at a time.
- Use everyday examples, comparisons, and step-by-step sequences.
- Prefer `왜 중요한가`, `예를 들면`, `해보기` blocks for learning.

## Certainty

- Do not oversimplify facts into false statements.
- Keep safety, legal, scientific, or source-backed limits visible in age-appropriate language.
- Avoid fear, shame, or exaggerated danger.

## Examples And Evidence

- Examples should clarify the concept, not replace the evidence.
- Keep numbers, source facts, names, and definitions accurate.
- If a term is necessary, define it simply instead of deleting it.

## Sentence Shape

- Short sentences are preferred.
- Use lists for steps.
- Use questions to guide attention, but do not overuse playful language in serious topics.

## Style-Pass Boundary

- Tone adjustment is `reader-fit` guidance for learning sequence and comprehension, not simplification at any cost.
- Use limited rewrite only on unprotected spans after marking protected spans.
- Run fidelity review before accepting edits; roll back or request human review if evidence-related, approval-sensitive, or genre-drift risk appears.
