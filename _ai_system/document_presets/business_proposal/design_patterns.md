# Business Proposal Design Patterns

## Document Purpose And Reader

- Purpose: Prepare a partner-facing or customer-facing proposal document.
- Primary readers: partner executives, customer decision makers, sponsors, procurement reviewers, or internal approvers.
- The document should clarify value, scope, roles, schedule, conditions, and the requested next decision. It is not a quotation document.

## Recommended Document Structure

- Partner/customer cover: recipient, sender, date, version, confidentiality, and proposal status.
- Recipient context: problem, opportunity, constraints, and decision need.
- Proposed value: proposed solution, expected benefit, evidence, and fit.
- Scope table: included work, deliverables, boundaries, dependencies, and acceptance basis.
- Role split table: sender, recipient, partner, vendor, and third-party responsibilities.
- Schedule: phases, milestones, decision gates, and assumptions.
- Conditions and exclusions box: out-of-scope items, commercial caveats, legal/procurement assumptions, and validity limits.
- Next step: requested decision, meeting, approval, pilot, or negotiation path.

## Recommended Layout Blocks

- Scope table with included/excluded columns.
- Role split table using a compact RACI-like structure when useful.
- Schedule table or timeline with milestones and owner.
- Terms/exclusions box that is visually separate from value claims.
- Evidence block for track record, references, or capability proof.

## Design Application Priorities

- Use a milestone timeline when sequence, decision gates, dependencies, or launch readiness matter more than a dense date table.
- Use a scope matrix when included work, excluded work, assumptions, deliverables, and acceptance basis need to be compared.
- Use R&R or RACI-style blocks when the proposal depends on client, partner, vendor, or third-party actions.
- Use assumptions, conditions, and exceptions blocks before pricing, schedule, or delivery promises can be mistaken as final commitments.
- Keep pricing summaries short and clearly labeled as proposal discussion material unless a separate quotation model governs the numbers.
- Split schedule, cost, and role tables when owner names, deliverables, or exception text would make the table too wide for Word.

## Tables, Figures, And Captions

- Scope, role, and schedule tables should be short enough to read in Word.
- Captions should make clear whether a table is a proposal commitment, assumption, or discussion item.
- Diagrams should support value, delivery flow, or responsibility alignment.
- Pricing option summaries may appear, but detailed line-item arithmetic belongs to a quotation model, not this proposal preset.

## AI Judgment Needed

- Decide whether each statement is a binding commitment, proposal assumption, negotiation item, or excluded scope.
- Decide whether a milestone timeline, scope matrix, R&R table, or assumptions block is the clearest first layout block for the recipient.
- Decide whether cost, schedule, and role information should be summarized in the body or separated into appendix-level detail.
- Decide whether wording could be misread as a final quotation, contract, SOW, acceptance, or invoice.

## Deferred Export-Native Features

- Do not claim automatic contract, quotation, SOW, signature, approval, or line-item pricing automation.
- Do not claim Word-native field codes, generated DOCX captions, page fields, or landscape sections for schedules and matrices.
- Treat native proposal templates, commercial document data models, and legal/procurement automation as separate work outside this module.

## Word/DOCX Compatibility

- Split large responsibility matrices and schedules into focused tables.
- Avoid interactive HTML, viewport-dependent layouts, complex absolute positioning, or web-only visual effects.
- Long deliverable names, terms, exclusions, and URLs must wrap cleanly.
- Use static diagrams/charts with captions and alternative descriptions.
- DOCX/PDF conversion still requires separate export validation; this pattern does not guarantee conversion success.

## Patterns To Avoid

- Treating the proposal as a formal quotation or invoice.
- Mixing binding commitments, optional ideas, assumptions, and exclusions without labels.
- Hiding legal, tax, procurement, approval, or validity caveats.
- Using internal brainstorming tone in a partner/customer document.

## Reviewer Checkpoints

- Are scope table, role split table, schedule, and terms/exclusions box present where relevant?
- Is the requested next decision clear?
- Are proposal commitments separated from assumptions and negotiation items?
- Is the boundary with quotation work explicit when pricing appears?
- Are tables and diagrams readable after Word/DOCX export?
- Are timeline, scope, cost, and responsibility blocks chosen for recipient clarity rather than document impressiveness?
