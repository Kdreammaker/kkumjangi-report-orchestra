# Education Curriculum Design Patterns

## Document Purpose And Reader

- Purpose: Create curriculum, lesson plans, instructor guides, learner handouts, activity sheets, assignments, or assessment packs.
- Primary readers: instructors, facilitators, curriculum reviewers, learners, training managers, or evaluators.
- The document should separate teaching guidance from learner-facing material and align objectives with activities and assessment.

## Recommended Document Structure

- Curriculum overview: learner profile, prerequisites, delivery mode, total duration, and completion criteria.
- Session table: lesson number, time, learning objective, topic, activity, material, assignment, and assessment.
- Instructor notes: facilitation script, timing cues, discussion prompts, expected misconceptions, and adaptation notes.
- Learner handouts: concise explanation, examples, activity instructions, worksheet space, and references.
- Activities and assignments: task, time, materials, output, and evaluation basis.
- Rubric/evaluation table: criteria, performance levels, scoring or qualitative feedback guide.
- Wrap-up and next steps: reflection, extension tasks, and follow-up materials.

## Recommended Layout Blocks

- 차시표/session table as the navigation spine.
- Instructor note boxes clearly marked for facilitator-only use.
- Learner handout sections that can be separated or printed.
- Activity cards with objective, time, materials, instruction, and output.
- Rubric/evaluation tables separated from teaching notes and handouts.

## Design Application Priorities

- Start with a teacher guide when facilitation choices, timing, misconceptions, discussion prompts, or adaptation notes drive quality.
- Start with a student handout when learners need printable instructions, examples, worksheet space, or reflection prompts.
- Use learning objective blocks when objectives must connect directly to activity, material, assessment, and reflection.
- Use activity cards for classroom, workshop, or training tasks that need time, grouping, materials, output, and evaluation basis.
- Use materials blocks when supplies, links, tools, preparation, or safety constraints affect delivery.
- Use assessment and reflection blocks when completion criteria, feedback, or learner self-check is part of the deliverable.
- For children, prefer shorter learner-facing text, larger visual anchors, clear task boundaries, and teacher-only facilitation notes kept out of the handout.

## Tables, Figures, And Captions

- Session tables should show progression and timing without becoming too wide.
- Rubrics should use concise criteria and split by competency if they grow dense.
- Concept diagrams need captions that connect to the learning objective.
- Handout figures should include labels learners can understand without instructor-only context.

## AI Judgment Needed

- Decide whether the current output is a teacher guide, student handout, mixed curriculum pack, assignment, or assessment pack.
- Decide which blocks are learner-facing and which must stay teacher-only.
- Decide whether child-facing language, visuals, and activity instructions need simplification without changing learning goals.
- Decide whether rubrics, session tables, or materials lists should be split by lesson, competency, activity, or audience.

## Deferred Export-Native Features

- Do not claim automatic teacher/student packet splitting, native worksheet generation, page fields, or Word-native running headers.
- Do not claim generated DOCX captions, native table-of-contents support, or automatic landscape sections for rubrics and session tables.
- Treat LMS packaging, printable worksheet automation, and native curriculum template automation as separate work outside this module.

## Word/DOCX Compatibility

- Keep 차시표, rubrics, and assessment tables within Word page width.
- Long activity prompts, rubric descriptors, material links, and URLs must wrap cleanly.
- Avoid interactive HTML, viewport-dependent layouts, complex absolute positioning, and web-only activity widgets.
- Use static diagrams/images with captions and alternative descriptions.
- DOCX/PDF conversion still requires separate export validation; this pattern does not guarantee conversion success.

## Patterns To Avoid

- Mixing instructor-only notes into learner handouts without labels.
- Objectives that cannot be assessed.
- Activities that do not connect to the lesson objective.
- Rubrics that are too wide or too vague to use.

## Reviewer Checkpoints

- Are 차시표, instructor notes, learner handouts, and rubric/evaluation tables visibly separated?
- Does each lesson align objective, activity, assignment, and assessment?
- Are timing, materials, and facilitation constraints practical?
- Are learner-facing materials readable without hidden instructor context?
- Are tables and handouts export-safe for Word/DOCX?
- Are child-facing and teacher-facing blocks visually and textually distinguished when both appear?
