# Press Release Design Patterns

## Document Purpose And Reader

- Purpose: Produce an external-facing press release, announcement, official statement, or media-ready draft.
- Primary readers: journalists, editors, external stakeholders, partners, customers, communications teams, legal reviewers, or executives.
- The document should be treated as a public release, not a condensed report.

## Recommended Document Structure

- Release status: immediate release, embargoed release, or review draft.
- Headline: newsworthy, factual, and specific.
- Subheadline: optional supporting detail or scope.
- Dateline: location and release date when applicable.
- Lead: the who, what, when, where, why, and significance in the opening paragraph.
- Body: approved details, context, product/service facts, partnership facts, or milestone explanation.
- Quote: approved spokesperson quote with name and title.
- Boilerplate: company or organization introduction.
- Contact: media contact, email, phone, website, or approved inquiry path.
- Approval notes: embargo, legal review, partner approval, and draft/public status.

## Recommended Layout Blocks

- Release status line at the top: `FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE`, `EMBARGOED UNTIL...`, or `DRAFT FOR REVIEW`.
- Headline and subheadline hierarchy before dateline.
- Short paragraphs for lead and body; avoid report-style sections unless needed.
- Quote block clearly separated from factual body text.
- Boilerplate and contact blocks at the end.

## Design Application Priorities

- Use `FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE` or `즉시 배포` only when facts, quotes, names, partner references, and timing are approved for public release.
- Use `EMBARGOED UNTIL...` or `엠바고` only when the embargo date, time zone, and recipient handling are explicit.
- Use review-draft status when public approval is incomplete, and keep internal rationale out of the release body.
- Place dateline before the lead when location and release date are part of the news convention.
- Use approved quote blocks only for named spokespersons with approved title, wording, and attribution.
- Use boilerplate, media contact, and fact box blocks only for public-approved company, product, contact, and data facts.

## Tables, Figures, And Captions

- Press releases usually do not need tables. Use only approved fact boxes or small data tables.
- Any image, fact box, or media-kit reference must be approved for external release.
- Captions should identify approved public context and should not expose internal source notes.
- Avoid report appendices, internal charts, and unapproved data artifacts.

## AI Judgment Needed

- Decide whether the release status is immediate, embargoed, review draft, or not ready for public drafting.
- Decide whether each fact, figure, partner reference, image, quote, and contact detail has approval for external use.
- Decide whether a fact box improves journalist readability or risks exposing internal metrics.
- Decide whether headline and lead language are newsworthy and factual without turning into unsupported marketing.

## Deferred Export-Native Features

- Do not claim automated press distribution, media-kit packaging, embargo enforcement, approval routing, or newsroom CMS publishing.
- Do not claim Word-native field codes, generated DOCX captions, page fields, or release-template automation.
- Treat public-release approval workflows, contact-list management, and external platform handoff as separate work outside this module.

## Word/DOCX Compatibility

- Keep fact boxes or small data tables within Word page width.
- Long company names, product names, spokesperson titles, contact details, and URLs must wrap cleanly.
- Use static approved images only; avoid interactive HTML, viewport-dependent layouts, and complex absolute positioning.
- Do not use web landing-page hero structures or viewport-height layouts.
- DOCX/PDF conversion still requires separate export validation; this pattern does not guarantee conversion success.

## Patterns To Avoid

- Treating the release as a report summary.
- Including internal rationale, unresolved risks, unapproved figures, or draft-only notes in the public body.
- Hype-heavy headline unsupported by approved facts.
- Quotes that are not attributed or approval-checked.
- Missing immediate release/embargo/review-draft status.

## Reviewer Checkpoints

- Is the status clear: immediate release, embargo, or review draft?
- Are headline, dateline, lead, body, quote, boilerplate, and contact in the correct public-document hierarchy?
- Are facts, figures, names, partner references, and quotes approved for external release?
- Is internal-only context excluded from the public body?
- Is the output Word/DOCX safe and free of web-only layout patterns?
- Are unapproved public statements, marketing exaggeration, and internal information exposure actively screened out?
